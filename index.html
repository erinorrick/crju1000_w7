CRJU 1000  ·  Week 7 Engagement Activity

Concept Mapping: Barriers to Reentry

25–30 minutes · SLOs 1, 2, 4 · Completion grade

What This Activity Is

A concept map is a visual way of laying out how different pieces of a system connect to each other. This week, the system is reentry: everything a person faces on the day they walk out of prison and back into the community.

The Scenario

A person has just been released after serving 5 years in prison for a nonviolent felony drug offense. They are heading back into their community today. What's waiting for them?

You'll build a map of at least six barriers this person might face, spread across three categories: Employment, Housing, and Civil & Legal Rights. For each barrier, you'll identify (1) which part of the system created or enforces it, and (2) which theory from the course best explains why it increases the likelihood of reoffending.

Before You Begin

Each barrier on your map needs two annotations. Here's what each one is asking:

System Actor Which part of the criminal justice system created or enforces this barrier? Choose from: law enforcement, courts, corrections, or legislation/policy. Many reentry barriers — like voting restrictions or public housing bans — come from legislation rather than from a specific agency's day-to-day operations.
Theory Which theory — labeling, strain, or social bond — best explains why this barrier makes reoffending more likely? You covered all three earlier in the course; this week's lecture (Part B) walks through how each one applies to reentry specifically.

Tip: If a theory connection doesn't click perfectly for a given barrier, pick whichever theory comes closest. The point of this activity is practicing the application — not finding one “correct” answer for every barrier.

How to Complete This Activity

1 Open the interactive mapping tool: Concept Mapping: Barriers to Reentry. It opens in a new tab.
2 Read the scenario, then build your map. Add barriers to each of the three categories using + Add Barrier. For each one, write a short description and select a system actor and a theory from the dropdowns. The tool tracks your progress and tells you what's still needed.
3 Once you have at least 6 complete barriers with at least one in each category, move on to the reflection question and write your two sentences.
4 On the final screen, use Copy Summary to copy a structured written version of your map and reflection.
5 Submit on this assignment page using any of the formats described below.

Submission — Choose Any Format

The interactive tool is one way to build your map, but it isn't the only way to submit it. Choose whichever of these three formats works best for you:

Structured Written List Use the interactive tool, then paste the Copy Summary output directly into the text-entry box on this assignment page.
Digital Diagram Build your map in a tool you already use — PowerPoint, Google Slides, Jamboard, Miro, or similar — with each barrier labeled by category, system actor, and theory. Upload the file or a screenshot/PDF.
Hand-Drawn Map Sketch your map on paper — central scenario, three branches, barriers with their annotations — and upload a clear photo.

Grading

This activity is graded on completion. To receive credit, your submission — in whatever format — needs to include:

☐ At least 6 barriers, with at least 1 in each of the 3 categories (Employment, Housing, Civil & Legal Rights)
☐ Every barrier has a system actor (law enforcement, courts, corrections, or legislation/policy)
☐ Every barrier has a theory (labeling, strain, or social bond)
☐ A two-sentence reflection on which barrier you'd address first, and why

A note on AI use: This activity works best as your own first attempt — the value is in practicing the connection between a real-world barrier and a theory you've learned. If you use AI tools at any stage, follow the course AI-use policy and be prepared to explain your reasoning for any annotation in your own words.
