Overview & Purpose

Assignment 5 asks you to run and interpret a multiple OLS regression model — writing the equation, interpreting partial slope coefficients, assessing model fit, making a prediction, and evaluating multicollinearity. This activity walks you through every one of those tasks using a different dataset so you arrive at Assignment 5 having already practiced the full workflow.

The output below comes from a model predicting recidivism risk scores for a sample of individuals released from prison. Work through each step carefully. There is calculation involved — keep a scratch pad handy.

Research Context A state department of corrections wants to understand what predicts validated recidivism risk scores among individuals released from prison. The outcome variable is risk_score (a continuous scale from 0–100; higher scores indicate greater predicted risk of reoffending within three years). Three predictors are included: age_release (age in years at release), prior_convictions (total number of prior felony convictions), and months_served (length of the most recent sentence in months). Data are from a random sample of n = 180 releases.
The Five Steps — Applied to Every OLS Model Step 1: Write the regression equation (Ŷ = a + b₁X₁ + … + bₖXₖ)
Step 2: Interpret the partial slope coefficients (b) — direction, magnitude, "holding constant"
Step 3: Assess overall model fit — F-test significance, R², Adjusted R²
Step 4: Make a prediction — plug specific X values into the equation
Step 5: Check multicollinearity — VIF values and what they mean

ColumnWhat it showsHow to use it
Coef. (b)Unstandardized partial slopeChange in risk_score per one-unit increase in that IV, holding all other IVs constant
Std. Err.Standard error of bPrecision of the estimate; used to compute the t-statistic
tt-statistic for each IVb / Std.Err. — how many SE's b is from zero
P>|t|Two-tailed p-valueIf p < .05 → reject H₀; that IV significantly predicts the DV

R-squaredProportion of DV variance explained by the full modelHigher = better fit; multiply by 100 for percentage
Adj R-squaredR² penalized for number of IVsMore honest than R²; use this for comparing models with different # of IVs
F(k, n-k-1)Overall model F-statisticTests whether the model as a whole explains significant variance
VIFVariance Inflation FactorVIF < 5: acceptable; 5–10: moderate concern; >10: serious multicollinearity
