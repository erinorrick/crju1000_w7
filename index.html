Concept Mapping: Barriers to Reentry

Step 1 of 4 — Scenario

Today's task is a concept map — a visual way of laying out how different pieces of a system connect to each other. Here, the system is reentry: what someone faces on the day they walk out of prison.

The Scenario

A person has just been released after serving 5 years in prison for a nonviolent felony drug offense. They are heading back into their community today. What's waiting for them?

Your Task

  • Build a map of at least 6 barriers this person might face, spread across three categories: Employment, Housing, and Civil & Legal Rights. Every category needs at least one barrier.
  • For each barrier, identify two things:
    • Which part of the system — law enforcement, courts, corrections, or legislation/policy — created or enforces this barrier.
    • Which theory — labeling, strain, or social bond — best explains why this barrier makes reoffending more likely.
  • Finish with a short reflection: if you were designing a reentry program, which barrier would you tackle first, and why?

Estimated time: 25–30 minutes. If a theory connection genuinely doesn't click for a barrier, pick the theory that comes closest — the goal is practicing the application, not finding a perfect fit every time.
